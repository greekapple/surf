# Map: a clean field when zoomed out, small number dots when close (user, 2026-11-12)

The user: "The map layout is getting better, but in Surfline, where it shows a number on a hued dot that reflects that spot, is cool. When you're zoomed out enough the map should be fully clean of any spots, and as you get close the dot idea comes in. The big chunky boxes with the name are just not minimalistic enough. Obviously give it your own twist; we shouldn't directly copy Surfline."

**Owner:** product-term (map). Next, before phone-pass items 8–12.
**Level:** 2–3. Data-honesty review is required, because the dot's number and colour are forecast claims.

## Zoom behaviour
| Zoom | Shows |
|---|---|
| Far out (≈ z < 6) | **Nothing but the field.** No pins, no group pills, no labels. The map is just the ocean field and coastline. |
| Coast (≈ z6–8.5) | **One small dot per coast group** (the existing 10–20-spot groups), at the group's centroid on the coast. The dot carries the group's *best* surf range midpoint as a number, coloured by the best spot's rating band. The group name only appears on hover, focus or tap. |
| Close (≈ z ≥ 8.5) | **One dot per spot.** Number = the spot's surf-range midpoint at the selected hour, in the user's unit (rounded; "<1" for under 1). Colour = its rating band, in our palette. The spot name appears on hover, focus or tap, and as a small label only from ≈ z11 and only when it doesn't collide. |
| Selected spot | The dot grows a little and gets the name label plus a ring. The existing callout/panel opens on tap as now. |

The thresholds are a starting point. Tune them so the field never looks busy: aim for at most about 25 dots on a phone screen. Collide dots against each other and drop the lower-rated one (it comes back as you zoom in).

## Our own twist (not Surfline's)
- **Swell-direction notch:** each dot has a tiny tick on its rim pointing where the dominant swell comes *from*. It's honest and useful at a glance, and it makes the dots ours. No tick when there is no swell partition.
- **The dot style says what kind of number it is.** No extra words are needed on the map, and the legend chip explains:
  - **solid fill + number** = our surf estimate (MOP or v1);
  - **thin dashed ring + number** = the NWS surf forecast (official, not ours);
  - **hollow ring, no number** = offshore-only. Never show the offshore height as a dot number;
  - **small grey ring** = not loaded yet or unrated;
  - **striped or faded** = the daily snapshot or a stale copy (keep the existing honest states).
- **Typography:** the number uses tabular figures and the Archivo type tokens, about 11–12 px in a 20–22 px dot. Text colour is picked per band for contrast in both themes.

## Remove
- The chunky group boxes and pills (name, Avg, best, count, bar), and the pin labels that are always on.
- Keep the information reachable: tapping a group dot opens a small sheet listing its spots (name, number, rating word, and the source kind as a short text). Tapping a spot dot opens the existing spot callout.

## Keep / honesty
- Every number on a dot is the same number the spot page shows for that hour, of the same kind. Add a test.
- The legend chip gets a "dots" key: estimate / NWS / offshore-only / not loaded.
- "After dark · not a surf window": dark hours show dots dimmed, not hidden.
- The background seaboard fill (map-fill) stays. Dots appear as data arrives.
- MapLibre gets a symbol or circle layer with feature-state updates (no DOM markers), which also helps audit B8 (scrub performance). Leaflet fallback: simple circle markers.
