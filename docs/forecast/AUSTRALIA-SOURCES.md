# Australia source readers (AODN wave buoys, Queensland TMR tide predictions), 2026-11-10
Code: `src/ocean/aodn.ts`, `src/ocean/tmrTides.ts`; tests: `src/tests/aodn-tmr.test.ts` with recorded fixtures in `src/tests/fixtures/australia/`. Study and licences: `docs/forecast/AUSTRALIA-DATA.md`; plan: `docs/specs/DRAFT-australia-wiring.md`. **Nothing is wired**: no Australian spot, no UI, no Worker route, no deploy, no Open-Meteo. **No Bureau of Meteorology data is read anywhere** (BoM terms: personal use only; the study's verdict is "not permitted" for display).

## 1. AODN / IMOS THREDDS wave buoys (`aodn.ts`)
- **Licence and credit:** CC BY 4.0 on every file (`license` attribute `http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/`). Credit in every value's notes: "Wave buoy data: <owner>, via the Australian Ocean Data Network (AODN), CC BY 4.0", the licence link, and the IMOS sentence verbatim (`IMOS_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT`: "Data was sourced from Australia’s Integrated Marine Observing System (IMOS) - IMOS is enabled by the National Collaborative Research Infrastructure Strategy (NCRIS)"). NSW DPE buoys add their own line (`NSW_DPE_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT`, passed per buoy as `extraAcknowledgement`).
- **Finding the file:** one NetCDF per month, and the name's start day is not always 01 (Rottnest's current file is `DOT-WA_20261102_...`), so `aodnCatalogURL` + `latestAodnFile` read the year folder's `catalog.xml` and take the newest `_monthly.nc`; `aodnYears` adds last year's folder in the first hours of January. Then the NCSS point CSV: `https://thredds.aodn.org.au/thredds/ncss/point/<urlPath>?var=<VARS>,WAVE_quality_control&time_start=<now-24h>&time_end=<now+1h>&accept=csv`. Variables by kind: Waverider owners (MHL, BoM, QLD DES, WA DoT, Gippsland, Pilbara) `WHTH,WPPE,WPDI,WPDS,WMXH`; Spotter owners (IMOS, NSW DPE, UWA, Deakin, Flinders, SA DEW) `WSSH,WPPE,WPDI,WPDS`.
- **Output** (`normalizeAodnBuoy`, `summariseAodn`): an `ObservationSet` with the context-buoy value names ("Offshore significant height" in ft from m, "Dominant wave period" = WPPE in s, "Peak wave direction" = WPDI, the direction the waves come from), plus `validAt`, `ageMinutes`, `cadenceMinutes` and `hsKind` ("zero-crossing" for WHTH, "spectral" for WSSH, named in the notes; never mixed). Columns are read by header name (the CSV has no station column).
- **Rules:** a row counts within 24 h of the retrieval and not in the future; `WAVE_quality_control` 3 (questionable), 4 (bad) and 9 (missing) drop the row; 1 reads "good", 2 "realtime, not quality-controlled" (every realtime row today). Each variable takes its own newest valid row (no filling across rows).
- **The Gold Coast fill:** QLD DES rows at :30 carry `WPPE=0.0, WPDI=0.0, WPDS=0.0, WMXH=0.0` beside a real `WHTH`. An exact 0.0 in those fields (and a height of 0.0) is **read as missing, never as 0**: Gold Coast's period and direction come from the :00 row (with their own times) while its height can come from the :30 row. A file with only fill rows is silent ("only fill rows").
- **WA DoT is 3-hourly and gappy:** the reader reports the buoy's cadence (median spacing: 180 min Rottnest, 30 Gold Coast, 60 MHL) and every value's notes say "Reports about every N minutes"; a 3.5 h old Rottnest reading is current for that buoy. Cape Naturaliste (1 row in 26 h at the study) is shown with its age (22 h 32 min in the recorded case) while it is within 24 h and is **silent** after, with its last row (`{ok: false, lastSeen, reason}`); `summariseAodn` lists every silent buoy with the reason.
- No swell/sea split exists in any realtime file; the buoy is context (not breaking-wave height) and the notes say so.
- **Fixtures** (recorded 2026-11-10 09:32Z): MHL Sydney, QLD Gold Coast (with the :30 fill rows), WA Cape Naturaliste (1 row), WA Rottnest (3-hourly), NSW DPE Tathra (Spotter, QC 1), and Rottnest's 2026 `catalog.xml`.

## 2. Queensland TMR predicted high/low tides (`tmrTides.ts`)
- **Licence:** each gauge's CKAN package is "Creative Commons Attribution 4.0" (`license_id CC-BY-4.0`, maintainer opendata@tmr.qld.gov.au). Credit: "Tide predictions: State of Queensland (Department of Transport and Main Roads), CC BY 4.0" (the record gives no stricter wording).
- **Access:** only the CKAN datastore API answers headless (the resource download URLs sit behind an AWS WAF challenge): `https://www.data.qld.gov.au/api/3/action/datastore_search?resource_id=<id>&filters={"Date":["DD/MM/YYYY",...]}`. Records: `Date`, `Time` (HH:MM), `Ind` ("1" high, "-1" low), `Reading` (metres, string).
- **Gauges** (`TMR_GAUGES`): Gold Coast Seaway (-27.9386, 153.4326) and Mooloolaba (-26.6843, 153.1334), positions from the DES storm-tide CSV. **A new resource is published every year** (2026 and 2027 exist today, ids in the registry and in the package record, `tmrResourceFromPackage`); a year with no table gives `{available: false}`, never last year's. Noosa Head has a set but no machine-readable position, so it is not listed.
- **Zone, stated honestly:** the record does **not** state the time zone. The times are the Brisbane clock (AEST, UTC+10, **no daylight saving**): the Seaway predictions for 2026-11-05/06 fall within 12-26 minutes of the DES storm-tide prediction series of the same gauge, whose clock is AEST (its `Seconds` epoch is 10 h behind its `DateTime`); a UTC reading would be 10 h off. The reader converts to ISO UTC instants and returns `zone: "Australia/Brisbane"` with a note saying this is inferred.
- **Datum, stated honestly:** the record does **not** state the datum (`datum: "not stated"`). The heights are metres above an unstated gauge datum (TMR tables are usually Lowest Astronomical Tide), matching the DES prediction level of the same gauge within 0.06 m. The notes say so and heights are not to be shown as chart-datum depths; times alone are safe. To confirm with opendata@tmr.qld.gov.au.
- Output: `TidePrediction[]` (ISO UTC, High/Low, height in ft with `nativeUnit` "m above an unstated gauge datum"), label "Queensland predicted tide at <gauge>".
- Fixtures: the datastore answers for Gold Coast Seaway and Mooloolaba (10-11 Nov 2026) and a slim Gold Coast Seaway package record.
