# Surf-height validation (T-089)

Generated by `docs/forecast/data/validate-surf-height.ts` on 2026-11-03T07:58Z. Refresh by rerunning it (it also appends today's live v2 headlines to `validation-archive.jsonl` and scores matured ones). Context for honesty, not a certification.

**What is scored.** The surf height we show is the nearshore significant wave height at the spot's 10 to 15 m depth (the surf-report convention, T-077) with a range built in `src/services/uncertainty.ts`. "Coverage" = how often the observed value fell inside the displayed whole-foot range; low coverage means the range is too narrow, very high coverage with a wide range means it is too wide. "Median abs error" is |observation − central|.

## 1. Backtest: what the models forecast N days ahead, past 14 days (33 spots with a MOP point and a breaking profile; 13900 spot-hour-lead rows, i.e. 3475 distinct spot-hours each scored at 4 leads; window = last 14 days of the feeds, fewer hours survive the MOP quality filter)
Forecast side: Open-Meteo Marine `previous_dayN` fields (what the model said N days earlier for that hour) through our v1 chain (offshore swell carried to 10 m by linear shoaling, total Hs); for leads of 1 day or more only the TOTAL wave height exists in the earlier runs, so the analysis swell is scaled by (total Hs forecast N days earlier ÷ total Hs analysis) (period and direction from the analysis) and the real range for that lead (no model-spread term, so slightly narrower than the app can show). The v2 live headline uses the MOP forecast as its centre; its own history only starts with the forward archive in section 2.

## Findings (stated plainly)
- Coverage against the MOP nowcast at lead 0 is 87% with the v1 range (which includes the ±17% offshore-to-10 m step) and 78% with the v2-style range (no step term), which is what spots with a MOP point actually show. At lead 5 days: 93% / 91%. Against the buoy-derived value (all leads): 84% / 74%. The range was designed around the central 80% of the wave-model error, so coverage above 80% means it is, if anything, wider than needed in this sample, and a v2-style coverage below 80% in a row would mean too narrow. These numbers do not contradict the range for this sample; they do not prove it.
- Sample size: 105 distinct hours per spot on average (the MOP nowcast quality filter and gaps leave far fewer than the 336 hours of 14 days), from 33 spots that share a few swell events and a handful of buoys, so the effective number of independent samples is small (a few events, not thousands of hours). No confidence interval is attached to the coverage figures.
- Median absolute error is flat across leads (0.5 ft at 0 days, 0.5 ft at 5). Partly this is an artefact of the reconstruction (leads 1 to 5 keep the analysis period and direction and scale only the Hs, which removes period and direction error), partly it is consistent with the spot transform and observation differences dominating in this fortnight. Coverage rising with lead is the range widening, not better forecasts.
- Relative to MOP, our central is about 14% high (observed MOP is 0.88 of it); relative to the buoy-derived value it is low (1.22). The two observations differ from each other by a median 46%, which is bigger than the bias being discussed, so neither source can settle it. This is NOT reconciled with the Ocean Beach finding in src/services/uncertainty.ts (header: MOP ÷ v1 median 1.06, v1 about 6% low, 782 hours, t008-pairs.csv; OB-BIAS-T076.md reports MOP ÷ buoy 0.95). This table averages 33 spots with guide-derived shelter and window assumptions and uses post-T-081 total Hs; the cause of the opposite sign is unknown (a guess: those spot assumptions). Ocean Beach (spot-2) is in the table like any other spot.
- Not yet measured: the live v2 headline at lead (section 2 fills in as hours mature, and compares a MOP forecast with the MOP nowcast, i.e. the same model family, not with reality), and surfer-reported heights (T-059 calibration is the only source for that).

## Reading this honestly
- Range design: half-width in ln units by lead (`LEAD_SKILL`: 0.16, 0.16, 0.17, 0.18, 0.24, 0.32, 0.36, 0.42 for 0 to 7 days) from the deep-water buoy error of the wave MODELS, rounded outward to whole feet. The coverage above is the test of whether that carries over to the transformed nearshore height.
- Fourteen days of the feeds in one season (fewer usable hours, see section 1); the backtest scores the v1 chain, not the live v2 headline (until section 2 matures). The buoy conversion reuses the same transform, so it checks the offshore-forecast error, not the transform's physics (OB-BIAS-T076.md covers that).
- Whole-foot ranges have a 1-ft minimum width, which flatters coverage for very small heights; the 3 ft+ table removes most of that.
